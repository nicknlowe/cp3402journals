# Journal 8

## Learning Activities
For this week, I focused on the impact of Time-Blocking, otherwise known as using a timer and avoiding distractions, to see if it improves my focus compared to open-ended sessions.

### Hypothesis
Setting a 20-minute timer where there are to be zero distractions will allow me to complete more script-writing for a mock group presentation.

### Test
1. I will choose to complete up to four script-writing tasks for similar slides, splitting it into two for the first method and the last two for the second method.
2. Method A - Open-Ended Session: For the first two tasks, I will work as I usually do without a timer block, checking my phone or Discord whenever I receive a notification.
3. Method B - Time-Blocking Session: For the last two tasks, I will set a 20-minute timer and commit to zero distractions until the alarm goes off.
4. After each method test, I will record the total time taken to complete both tasks and note any additional details like the number of times I got distracted.

### Result
After conducting the method A test of an open-ended session, I found it took me 35 minutes to complete two scripts, thanks to the three times I got distracted replying to people on Discord. In contrast, I managed to finish both tasks in just 22 minutes when under the time-blocking conditions due to the restrained environment forcing me to stay disciplined. Therefore, the hypothesis is true: setting a structured time limit to reduce distractions can significantly boost my focus leading to work completed in a faster timeframe.

## Learning Insights
Through this experiment, I have learnt that self-restraint and time management are just as critical as technical skills when working in any environment. By applying a structured method like time-blocking, I am developing the ability to remain focused, allowing me to complete the assigned work within a shorter period of time. This discipline ensures that I can remain productive and meet deadlines without being constantly distracted, which is a key trait that many employers are looking for in any given IT sector.
