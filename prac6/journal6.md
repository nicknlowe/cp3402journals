## Content Insights

For this sixth week, I learnt the importance of establishing good practices to successfully manage a group project, specifically through regular communication and collaborative methods like stand-up meetings and the use of GitHub for version control. This approach ensures that team members have a shared understanding on where the project is and that the project's codebase and files remain accessible and stable throughout the development process. Without the establishment of these frameworks, a project risks falling into disorganisation, with team members forced to work in an isolated environment along with the new added risk of data loss or conflicting codebases due to a lack of communication.

The use of stand-up meetings provides a dedicated space to catch-up on progress, identify any potential issues and how the team will continue to proceed thanks to the three main questions:
* What did you do yesterday?
* Are there any impediments in your way?
* What will you do today?

In addition to communication measures, technical collaborative tools like GitHub are employed for version control, allowing developers in a team to contribute to the same repository simultaneously without the risk of overwriting each other's work and the ability to rollback to a previous version. This is achieved through the use of branching and merging, which allows each developer to work on features in isolation before integrating their changes into the main codebase. By using pull requests, the team can also perform code reviews to ensure quality and consistency before any new code additions are finalised.

## Career/Employability/Learning Insights

After setting up our groups, I wanted to understand just how important employers, particularly software related, viewed good teamwork and communication skills and whether or not applying these learnt practices could prove essential in my employability. Upon reviewing job openings on websites like Seek and Indeed, I discovered that these skills are almost always listed as mandatory or desired requirements, often carrying as much weight as technical proficiency in programming languages. Additionally, many employers were also seeking established skills in version control and code reviews to ensure that new hires can be integrated into the existing development workflow.

By simulating a professional project development environment with the help of tools typically utilised by employers like Slack, Discord, Trello and GitHub, I am directly improving on these skills that organisations are non-negotiably looking for and demonstrating my ability to work in a team both socially and technically. It is through this hands-on experience with collaboration and version control tools that will allow any potential future employers to actively review my contributions, ultimately increasing my employability for a career within the industry.
